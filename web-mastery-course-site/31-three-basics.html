Lesson 31 — Three.js: scene, camera, renderer

Three.js wraps WebGL so you can build 3D worlds with a friendly object model. Everything starts with three objects: a scene to hold things, a camera to look from, and a renderer to draw.

Before you startThese demos load Three.js from a CDN, so you need an internet connection and a browser with WebGL. If the preview is blank, check the browser console and your hardware-acceleration setting.

The big picture

  • Scene: the root of a tree (scene graph) of objects.
  • Mesh = geometry (shape: vertices) + material (surface look). Other objects: lights, cameras, groups, helpers.
  • Camera: the viewpoint and projection (PerspectiveCamera mimics an eye; OrthographicCamera has no perspective).
  • Renderer (WebGLRenderer): draws the scene as seen by the camera onto a <canvas>, ideally 60 times per second.

Coordinates: right-handed. +X right, +Y up, +Z toward you. Units are arbitrary: pick a scale (1 unit = 1 meter is common) and stay consistent.

The same thing in a real project

Instead of appending the canvas to body, create your own <canvas id="scene"> and pass it: new THREE.WebGLRenderer({ canvas, antialias: true }). Style it with CSS like any element (width:100%; height:100dvh; display:block).

Cameras in detail

ParameterMeaning
fovVertical field of view in degrees. 35–75 typical; lower = flatter/zoomed, higher = wide-angle distortion.
aspectwidth / height of the canvas.
near, farClipping planes. Anything closer or farther isn't drawn. Keep the ratio modest (not 0.0001 to 100000) or you'll get z-fighting flicker.

Common beginner problems

  • Black screen: camera inside the object or pointing away; forgot renderer.render; a lit material with no lights; object outside near/far.
  • Stretched image after resize: forgot camera.aspect + updateProjectionMatrix().
  • Blurry on retina: forgot setPixelRatio.
  • Always open the Console; Three.js logs helpful warnings.

Exercises

  1. Replace the box with a sphere, cone, torus and cylinder. Find the constructor arguments in the docs (threejs.org/docs).
  2. Add three cubes at different positions, each a different MeshBasicMaterial color.
  3. Move the camera so you look straight down on the grid (camera.position.set(0, 8, 0) + lookAt).
  4. Change fov from 20 to 120 and describe the effect.
  5. Swap to an OrthographicCamera and note what disappears.
  6. Make the cube's rotation speed depend on mouse X using a pointermove listener.

Checkpoint

What do you need to render?A scene, a camera, a renderer, and something visible in the scene.
Why call updateProjectionMatrix()?The camera caches its projection math. After changing fov, aspect, near or far you must recompute it.
