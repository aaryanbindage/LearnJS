Project 4 — Interactive 3D scene

Build a polished, performant, interactive 3D experience. Choose vanilla Three.js or React Three Fiber. Doing it once in each is the best way to learn both.

Pick a concept

  • Product configurator: a glTF object (shoe, chair, headphones) on a turntable. UI to change colors/materials, hotspots with labels, screenshot button.
  • Low-poly island / diorama: stylized world with water shader, instanced trees, day/night cycle, ambient animation, and orbit camera limits.
  • Mini-game: marble maze, endless runner, or tower-stacker with physics, score, restart, and sound.
  • Data visualization: 3D globe or city with data-driven bars/points, hover tooltips, filters and animated transitions.

Requirements

  1. Scene quality: environment lighting, shadows or baked AO, tone mapping, consistent color management, and a considered palette.
  2. Interaction: raycast hover + click feedback; keyboard and touch support; camera constraints.
  3. Motion: delta-time animation, easing, and at least one scripted camera move.
  4. Custom shader: at least one ShaderMaterial or onBeforeCompile effect (water, dissolve, fresnel glow, gradient sky...).
  5. Assets: at least one optimized glTF (Draco/Meshopt) and compressed textures; loading screen with progress.
  6. Post-processing: at most 2–3 passes, with a toggle for low-power mode.
  7. UI: HTML/CSS overlay using the skills from Parts 2–5 (responsive, accessible, keyboard-operable). Real text for any important information.
  8. Performance: ≥ 55 FPS on a mid-range laptop and ≥ 30 FPS on a phone; < 150 draw calls; documented budgets and measurements (renderer.info).
  9. Robustness: resize, DPR cap, context-loss handling, WebGL fallback, pause when hidden, proper disposal, reduced-motion mode.
  10. Delivery: deployed URL, README with GIF/screenshots, architecture notes, and a "what I'd do next" list.

Suggested architecture (vanilla)

Each module exposes a small class with update(dt) and dispose(). The loop calls update on everything registered.

Milestones

  1. Block-out: primitives, camera, lights, controls. Lock the composition first.
  2. Assets: model, textures, environment. Optimize and load with progress.
  3. Interaction: raycasting, selection, camera transitions, UI hooks.
  4. Look-dev: materials, custom shader, post-processing, color grading.
  5. Performance & polish: profile, budgets, mobile test, fallback, accessibility, cleanup.
  6. Ship: build, deploy, document.

Self-review

Questions a reviewer would ask
  • What's your draw-call and triangle count? What did you do to reduce them?
  • What happens on a phone, with WebGL disabled, or when the tab is in the background?
  • Can a keyboard-only user, or a screen-reader user, get the essential information?
  • Does anything leak after navigating away and back (memory, listeners, loops)?
  • Can you explain every shader line?
