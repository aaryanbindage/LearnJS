Lesson 38 — Physics, particles & post-processing

Make the world behave, fill it with thousands of things cheaply, and finish it with a cinematic look.

Physics

Three.js draws; it doesn't simulate. Pair it with a physics engine that keeps its own world of invisible bodies, then copy each body's position and rotation to its mesh every frame.

  • cannon-es: simple, pure JS. Rapier (@dimforge/rapier3d-compat): fast WebAssembly, more features. In R3F use @react-three/rapier (declarative <RigidBody>).
  • Concepts: world + gravity, rigid bodies (dynamic, static, kinematic), colliders/shapes, materials (friction, restitution), forces/impulses, collision events.
  • Step the simulation with a fixed time step for stability: world.step(1/60, dt, 3).

Live demo with cannon-es: click to drop boxes and spheres onto a ground.

Particles and instancing

  • THREE.Points: each vertex becomes a square/sprite. Great for stars, dust, smoke, data viz with 100k+ points. Animate in the vertex shader (cheap) rather than in JS.
  • InstancedMesh: draw N copies of one mesh in a single draw call, each with its own transform/color. Use it for forests, crowds, bricks, grass: thousands of meshes at the cost of one.
  • Draw calls (one per mesh+material) are usually the first bottleneck, not triangles.

Post-processing with bloom (live)

Post-processing renders the scene to a texture, then applies full-screen effects (bloom glow, depth of field, SSAO, color grading, film grain, outlines). With the EffectComposer, passes run in order. Glowing pixels must be brighter than the bloom threshold.

In R3F use @react-three/postprocessing: <EffectComposer><Bloom luminanceThreshold={1} mipmapBlur /><DepthOfField /><Vignette /></EffectComposer>. Each pass costs fill-rate: budget them, and test on phones.

Exercises

  1. Add a static wall of boxes and shoot spheres at it by clicking (apply an impulse from the camera direction with body.applyImpulse).
  2. Build a Plinko / marble run with static pegs and dynamic balls.
  3. Replace the 120 individual meshes in the physics demo with one InstancedMesh.
  4. Animate the galaxy in its vertex shader (swirl by radius and time) instead of rotating on the CPU.
  5. Add BokehPass or an outline pass for the hovered object (from Lesson 35).
  6. Build a particle fountain: spawn, update and recycle 5,000 points using a typed array and needsUpdate.

Checkpoint

Why a fixed physics time step?Variable steps make simulations unstable and non-deterministic. A fixed step with accumulated sub-steps keeps behavior consistent across frame rates.
Why is instancing faster?It issues one draw call for all copies; each draw call has CPU overhead, so thousands of separate meshes bottleneck on the CPU.
