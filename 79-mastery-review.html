The 10 questions you should answer out loud

Explain each to an imaginary colleague in under two minutes, with an example. If you stumble, that's your study list.

  1. What happens between typing a URL and seeing a page, including the critical rendering path?
  2. How does the browser compute layout, and what triggers reflow vs repaint vs composite?
  3. Walk through the event loop with promises, timers, rAF and rendering.
  4. What makes a React render happen, what does reconciliation do, and how do you stop wasted work?
  5. Where should each kind of state live in a large React app, and why?
  6. How does a frame get from renderer.render() to pixels? What are draw calls, and what limits performance?
  7. How do you secure a web app against XSS, CSRF and broken access control?
  8. How would you design the data model, API and caching for a product like Trello?
  9. How do you deploy a change safely with zero downtime, and how would you know it broke something?
  10. Describe a hard bug you found by debugging systematically, and what you changed to prevent it.

Exercises

  1. Complete the checklist honestly. For every gap, schedule a focused session: reread the lesson, redo the exercises, and build one small project that forces the skill.
  2. Record yourself answering three of the ten questions. Watch it back, and note unclear explanations and filler.
  3. Pair with someone (or a rubber duck) and teach one hard topic: teaching exposes gaps faster than rereading.
  4. Take a past project and do a "ruthless review" with the rubrics from the clean code and testing & quality gates lessons: list ten concrete improvements and make five.

Checkpoint

How do you know you've mastered something?You can build it from scratch without references, explain why it works and when it fails, debug it when it breaks, and teach it to someone else.
