Lesson 35 — Interaction: raycasting

The DOM knows what you clicked; a 3D scene doesn't. A raycaster shoots an invisible line from the camera through the pointer and tells you which objects it hits.

How it works

  1. Convert the pointer position to normalized device coordinates (NDC): x and y from −1 to +1, with y pointing up.
  2. raycaster.setFromCamera(pointer, camera) builds a ray.
  3. raycaster.intersectObjects(objects, recursive) returns hits sorted by distance: object, point, distance, face, uv, instanceId.

Hover and click (live)

Hover a box to highlight it; click to launch it upward; click the floor to place a marker.

Performance and patterns

  • Raycast against a short list of interactive objects, not scene.children with recursive = true, and not every frame on every pointer event if the scene is big. Throttle or do it in the loop once per frame (as above).
  • For complex models use invisible low-poly proxy meshes (or three-mesh-bvh for accelerated raycasts).
  • InstancedMesh hits give hit.instanceId.
  • Use layers to include/exclude objects: raycaster.layers.set(1).

Exercises

  1. Click a box to select it (outline with a second slightly larger back-face mesh, or an EdgesGeometry line). Click empty space to deselect.
  2. Click the floor to move a character marker there smoothly.
  3. Drag boxes along the floor using pointerdown/move/up and plane intersection (disable OrbitControls while dragging).
  4. Show an HTML tooltip (a real DOM element) next to the hovered object using .project(camera).
  5. Make 10,000 instanced cubes and report which instance is under the mouse.

Checkpoint

Why flip the Y coordinate?DOM Y grows downward; Three's NDC Y grows upward, from -1 (bottom) to +1 (top).
How do I stop orbit drags from counting as clicks?Compare pointer-down and pointer-up positions (or time) and ignore the click if the pointer moved more than a few pixels.
